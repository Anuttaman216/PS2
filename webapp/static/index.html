01 · The challenge

Today, every cargo is a new bet on a volatile market.

CAPESIZE TCE · 104 WEEKSDaily
each red tick = a single spot fixture negotiated from scratch
A

Reactive, daily market exploration

Coal parcels are chartered one by one on the spot market, a few weeks before loading. Nothing tells the desk whether today's rate is a peak or a trough, so it cannot pick its moment to enter the market.

B

A market that swings wildly

Dry-bulk freight is among the most volatile prices in the world. In two years the Capesize rate moved by ~100% between trough and peak. One badly timed fixture can erase a quarter's savings.

C

Ports that decide the ship

East Coast drafts range from 8.0 m at Haldia to 19.5 m at Gangavaram. The wrong vessel size means part-cargoes, lighterage, idle berths and demurrage, and the draft also changes with the monsoon.

D

Too many single spot fixtures

On the pilot lane the daily-spot baseline needed 268 separate spot fixtures in 5½ years. The Ministry's objective is to move to short and medium-term multi-voyage contracts.

02 · Why it matters

Freight is a steel-making cost. Treat it like one.

SAIL imports millions of tonnes of coking coal each year. Every dollar per tonne of freight goes straight into the cost of hot metal. A rigorous, forecast-driven chartering strategy brings lower cost and more predictable supply.

03 · Port-vessel feasibility, live

Will it fit? Pick a port and watch the ships settle.

Uses the same physics as the engine: cargo at draft = DWT − TPC × 100 × (design draft − allowed draft) − constants. A green ship loads full, an amber ship is part-laden to the port draft, and a red ship cannot call.
